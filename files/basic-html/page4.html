Magazine hunting quickly became a ritual. Every pay-
                                                                  check became an excuse to convince my older brother
                                                                  to drive us to our local Barnes & Noble, where I would
                                                                  nearly empty my wallet moments after walking through
                                                                  the doors. At the time, I had no intention of starting a
                                                                  magazine. I was content being a connoisseur—absorbing
                                                                  writing on our collective social, political climates; indulging
                                                                  artists’ processes; gathering fashion inspiration; and lin-
                                                                  gering over photography that honored its subjects as
                                                                  much as their image.
                                                                  When I ﬁ rst entertained the idea of turning my notebook
                                                                  into a magazine, I imagined I’d have time for it decades
                                                                  later. Arbitrary, but it made sense to me then. I put a pin
                                                                  in the idea as I ﬁ nished college, though I continued ﬁ lling
                                                                  the journal’s pages, committed to being as honest as pos-
                                                                  sible in my writing.
                                                                  As life would have it, I spent the next two and a half years
                                                                  bouncing around the East Coast, still collecting expe-
                                                                  riences in that same notebook whenever I could spare
                                                                  moments between countless odd jobs, befriending won-
                                                                  derful people, evading predatory landlords, trying to make
                                                                  space for creative work, and the occasional lover. Most of
                                                                  the time, I was more focused on balancing responsibili-
                                                                  ties than giving my creative ideas consistent attention. I
                                                                  let life carry me forward until a brief affair in the fall of 2024
                                                                  became a quiet wake-up call. By the time it ended the fol-
                                                                  lowing spring, 2025, I found myself returning to passions
                                                                  I had slowly drifted away from, and the magazine came
                                                                  back into focus.
                                                                  Though my life grew busier than ever as I took on a
                                                                  second job to make ends meet, I settled into a peaceful
                                                                  but demanding rhythm I hadn’t experienced before. With
                                                                  less free time, I began cutting away what no longer served
                                                                  me, focusing instead on what mattered most.
